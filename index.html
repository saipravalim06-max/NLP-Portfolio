NLP IN EVERYDAY LANGUAGE

Where the concepts show up in real life

NEOLOGISM
doomscrolling

A word that did not exist a few years ago. In class I said new words appear every day.

IN NLPA model trained on older text may never have seen the word, so it cannot place its meaning.

INFORMAL LANGUAGE
brb · idk · tbh

Texting shortens words and drops letters.

IN NLPA system has to expand these (be right back, I don’t know) before it can read the message properly.

AMBIGUOUS WORDS
bat: animal / sports equipment

One spelling, two unrelated meanings.

IN NLPThe system has to pick the right sense. This is called word sense disambiguation.

CONTEXT · THE FIX
river bank / bank loan

The words around a word tell you which meaning is meant.

IN NLPModels read the neighbouring words, which is why contextual embeddings like BERT beat fixed word vectors.

LANGUAGE VARIATION
Same idea, different word order
Telugu · Subject – Object – Verb
నేనుI · subjectఅన్నంrice · objectతింటానుeat · verb
English · Subject – Verb – Object
Isubjecteatverbriceobject

Languages arrange the same ideas in different orders. Telugu puts the verb last.

IN NLPA translator cannot swap words one by one. It has to rearrange the whole sentence.

REGIONAL LANGUAGES
22 scheduled languages

India has many languages, and a lot of them have far less data than English.

IN NLPThis is why models like IndicBERT and IndicTrans2 were built, to close the gap.

POETRY · TELUGU

ఉప్పు కప్పురంబు

Uppu Kappurambu · Yogi Vemana · Vemana Padyalu

Tap a line to read it

The idea

Salt and camphor are both white crystals, so they look the same. Taste them and they are nothing alike. People are the same: judge by character, not by appearance.

Context

Vemana wrote short moral verses in everyday Telugu. Each one ends with the signature line “Viswadabhirama Vinura Vema”, which names him as the poet.

Literal meaning (word for word)

Salt and camphor stay with one likeness. Look, look: the trace of tastes is different. Among men, virtuous men are different. Viswadabhirama, listen, Vema.

LLM-generated meaning

Salt and camphor look alike, yet look again and their tastes part ways. So it is with people: the truly good stand apart. Listen, Vema.

47%Lexical similarity (word-overlap cosine)
HighSemantic similarity: both carry the same moral
AccuracyFluencyNote
Literal5 / 53 / 5Keeps every word, but reads stiffly in English.
LLM4 / 55 / 5Reads naturally, but drops the address to Viswadabhirama.
POET · YOGI VEMANASOURCE · VEMANA PADYALUERA · 17TH–18TH C. (DATES UNCERTAIN)THEME · APPEARANCE VS CHARACTER

Why it belongs in NLP: the poem is a lesson in meaning ≠ words. A translator has to carry the image of two look-alike crystals, not just the vocabulary, or the moral disappears.

FEATURED PROJECT · TEAM OF THREE

Compliance Intelligence Platform

An NLP-based approach to contract compliance and state policy analysis. It combines NLP with rule-based reasoning to check contract clauses against internal policies and state regulations, and shows the supporting clause as evidence.

InputProfile, contract, policies
→
SplitClauses and requirements
→
RetrieveEmbeddings find related text
→
CompareRules check numbers and permissions
→
VerdictA label with the evidence
MODULE 1

State Fit Advisor

Evaluates a company profile against 93 state policy requirements and marks each one PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN or POTENTIAL.

MODULE 2

Contract vs Internal Policies

Finds clauses that clash with internal policy. Example: a 30-day termination notice against a 60-day minimum.

MODULE 3

Contract vs State Regulations

Retrieves relevant regulations and flags possible conflicts for legal review, with source references.
